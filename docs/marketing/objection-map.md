_Gate B input. Built Sep 2026. **Read the sample caveat at the bottom before using this to
justify anything.** These are category objections from published journalism, not verbatims from

## Where this came from, and what it is not

HalalGoes is pre-launch: no reviews, no support tickets, no lost-deal log, no ad comments, no
waitlist. An objection map invented from our own page would be our assumptions with a table
around them, so this is not that.

What it is: consumer and expert language from **one CBC Marketplace investigation** —
*"Fast-food chains serving up halal food with a side of misinformation, expired certificates"*,
Charlsie Agro and Madeline McNair, CBC News, 18 October 2024. The team went undercover at ten
locations of KFC, Popeyes, Boston Pizza, Osmow's and Mary Brown's, with a Muslim employee who
follows a halal diet asking the questions Muslim consumers and certification experts told them
they need answered before feeling comfortable sitting down to eat.

These are objections about **the category**, not about us. That is a real limitation and it is
restated at the bottom.

### Documented findings (not quotes — the investigation's own results)

These are not customer language, so they are not objections. They are the evidence that the
objections are rational, and they map onto our checks one for one.

| CBC Marketplace finding | The check it corresponds to |
|---|---|
| Employees at **six of the ten** locations said the entire restaurant was certified halal — **not one of the ten was** | **H6_SCOPE_SUFFICIENT** — scope covers everything sold here |
| Staff showed a certificate issued to a **meat supplier**, used to imply the restaurant was certified | **H3_NAME_MATCH** — certified legal name matches this restaurant |
| **Eight expired certificates** shown during the visits; one set had expired **eight years** earlier | **H5_DATES_VALID** — dates valid today, not about to lapse |
| **Four of ten** could not say whether meat was hand- or machine-slaughtered, or gave wrong information | **H6_SCOPE_SUFFICIENT** |
| **More than a dozen** certifying agencies in Canada, each with its own policies and oversight, **unregulated**; the CFIA requires certification but does not certify and does not regulate certifiers | **H2_ISSUER_ACCEPTED** — issuing body on our accepted registry |

**This is the single most important fact for the copy:** the seven checks were not designed
against a hypothetical. Every failure mode CBC documented is a check we already run. That is not
a claim we need to make about ourselves — it is a correspondence a reader can verify.

## Objections the page never answers

Ranked by how directly they block a waitlist signup:

1. **"It just saves you the hassle of asking again and again."** The strongest and most
   under-served. Our copy sells *our process*; the customer wants *their effort back*. Nothing on
   the page currently says "you can stop interrogating waitstaff."
2. **"It is very unfortunate that this is something that is prevalent in the industry."** Nothing
   establishes why a verification layer needs to exist. A cold visitor who has never been misled
   does not know what problem we solve.
3. **Slaughter method (hand vs machine).** Named explicitly by the HMA as a trust-breaking
   detail. Our check 06 covers scope generically; the method is not surfaced anywhere.
4. **"Will you spam me?"** — not in this source, because this source is about restaurants, not
   waitlists. It is the *actual* objection to our actual ask and we have no evidence on it.

## Believed but not evidenced

- We assumed the certificate/seal *mechanism* is what persuades. The verbatims suggest the
  persuasive thing is **relief from vigilance** — the emotional register is exhaustion, not
  curiosity about process. The seven-check instrument is the proof; it is probably not the hook.
- "0% commission at launch" (the restaurant hook) appears nowhere in this evidence. It may still
  be right — restaurants are a different audience — but nothing here supports it.
- Nothing in this evidence is about **riders** at all. The third audience is entirely unevidenced.

## Decision

**Pull more sources before treating any of this as settled** — but do not block Gate B on it. The
copy can proceed against the category evidence with the framing above, because the pre-launch ask
is narrow (a phone number) and its objections are structural. Revisit the moment there are real
waitlist replies, which is the first genuine source we will own.

## Sample caveat

**Nine quotes and five findings, from one investigation of ten locations, published October
2024.** That is a very small sample and it is about the restaurant category, not about
HalalGoes, not about food delivery, and not about our waitlist. Frequency counts are meaningless at
this size and none are claimed. This supports *direction* — what register to write in, which
fears are real, that the seven checks correspond to documented failures. It does not support any
claim about how our prospects will behave, and it must not be cited as though it did.

## Using this in copy — the legal line

The investigation is public and attributable, and the copy may reference its findings. But **we
cite CBC; we never make the accusation ourselves**, and we do not name the chains. "A 2024 CBC
Marketplace investigation found eight expired halal certificates, one expired by eight years"
is reportage. The same sentence with a brand name attached, on a competitor-adjacent commercial
page, is disparagement exposure for no persuasive gain. Invariant #8 also applies: we describe
what was documented, never issue a religious ruling about anyone.
