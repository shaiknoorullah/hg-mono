01 / SELECTION

Six plans entered. One survived.

Six genuinely different operating models were designed independently, each grounded in real methodology research and each required to self-critique. Three independent Fable judges then scored them on conflicting mandates, so they could not converge politely. The incumbent plan — the lead agent's own — was entered on equal terms and lost.

Lens 1 — Delivery risk
C > A > E > F > B > D
Won by C: the only plan with real value in production in week one. Punished machinery on the critical path — D scored 2/10, B 3/10.
Lens 2 — Correctness power
B > F > D > E > C > A
Judged empirically against 10 real Halal Goes defects. Before-merge catches: B 7, F 7, D 6, C 5, E 5, A 3.5.
Lens 3 — Review bandwidth
B > F > C > D > E > A
Won by B: verification cost scales with hardware, not model calls. A finished last (4.0) — its review question was unanswerable.

Why the incumbent lost

Plan A's verification sat inside the trust boundary: the agent that wrote the code also wrote the test that blessed it. Its headline gate was diff-coverage — and all ten real defects would have passed it. They compiled, type-checked, and had green tests. The tournament did its job: it falsified the lead agent's first draft instead of decorating it.

02 / THESIS

Own the front door on day four

The scarce resources are time-to-real-user and human judgment, in that order. So the spine is the plan that owns production routing in week one, where every increment is live, measured, and reversible with a file write — and whose cancellation story is good at every week.

But the strangler spine is a superb migration verifier and a mediocre correctness system: its diff oracle is the defective legacy system, so it structurally canonizes bugs like client-supplied pricing. So onto that spine we bolt the cheapest, highest-catch-power mechanical gates from the other five plans — the ones that are model-independent, cost tens of lines, and catch what a diff oracle structurally cannot.

The governing principle, taken from the correctness judge: any gate that reduces to "an agent writes tests and CI runs them" gets zero credit. Every load-bearing gate here is either exogenous (production traffic), structural (the schema makes the bug unrepresentable), or authored by a different party than the implementer.

03 / MECHANISMS

The seven that define it

Every mechanism is boring, small, and traceable to a defect that actually happened.

Take the front door

Traefik + a Go "Fig" proxy at 100% passthrough by day ~4 — zero behavior change. It becomes recorder, contract-generator, shadow-diff engine, and identity broker. Week one ships emergency authz on the wide-open admin/refund routes and WebSocket identity enforcement: real user protection before any scaffolding exists.

from C · 8-step ladder: observe → shadow → canary → contract

The Stepper — Temporal's replacement

A ~400-line in-repo Postgres saga package. deadline_at NOT NULL makes the legacy wait-forever bug unrepresentable; per-step idempotency keys make retries safe by construction; state of any order is one SQL query. Zero new infrastructure. It shadows Temporal read-only for weeks, then actuates new orders only — no running order is ever migrated.

from F (schema) + C (shadow-then-actuate) · River/DBOS are named escape hatches, not defaults

The Bug Ledger

All ~30 known legacy defects committed as failing tests before the corresponding feature is written. The regression suite starts non-empty on day one. Every escaped defect and every human rejection joins it permanently.

merges four plans' duplicate ideas (museum / bestiary / counterexamples) into one corpus

Cheap structural gates

Config-reality probes (/debug/deps compares resolved-connected addresses against the manifest; a localhost-literal lint; fail-loud on missing env) — ~11 lines each, and they kill the redis://localhost:6379-in-Docker class outright. Plus server-authoritative money middleware, generated typed clients, stub-sniffer, assertion-count.

from F + D + E · each catches a specific real defect

The Closure Ledger — invented here

A generated Actor × Terminal-State matrix. Every cell demands a passing test or a human-signed n/a: rider × DELIVERED → a test proving an earnings row exists. Nobody has to imagine "did we pay the rider?" — the matrix emits the cell mechanically. Backed by a nightly query asserting every order's cash fully decomposes across all party ledgers, and a lint failing any UI input that maps to no RPC.

solves the defect class no plan could catch

Dated pilot — week 10

One real restaurant, 3–5 riders, ~30 invited customers, live paid orders 100% through the new stack. Not staging, not shadow — money moves. A value-ordered scope-cut list is signed in week 6 so schedule pressure lands on scope, never on gates.

solves the delivery blind spot: no plan put a real user on the critical path

Maintaining the reviewer

A weekly State-of-the-System briefing generated from repo facts so it cannot drift, keeping your global model current enough for local answers to mean anything. Plus monthly seeded defects aimed at you: miss 2 of any rolling 4 and review is declared decayed — auto-merge freezes until your catch rate recovers.

solves the bandwidth blind spot: every plan audited the agents, none audited the human

05 / HUMAN PROTOCOL

Your ten minutes a day

A twice-daily digest with at most two card types. One question each, a pre-selected default, and silence is a decision.

Promotion Card

Traffic-weight changes. Shows shadow hours, requests, users, mismatch rate with every diff classified, latency delta, and the rollback command with its drill date.

"Is this measured risk acceptable?"
default: approve · ~90 seconds

PR Card (hazardous lane only)

Five lines: WHAT / WHY / BREAKS / CHECKED / ROLLBACK. Any load-bearing adjective — idempotent, atomic, authorized — must cite a test ID or the card fails lint.

"Does this match what we want the product to do?"
default: approve · silence merges in 24h

Never "is this code correct." The machine certifies conformance; you certify intent and risk — the two things only you can judge. Waiting is productive: a card's evidence keeps accumulating while it sits.

Lanes: ~60% of PRs auto-merge unread; ~30% get gates plus a card you may skip; ~10% (money, auth, PII, order state machine, migrations, edge) need an explicit yes. 5% of auto-merges are randomly sampled into your digest for real reading — the audit that stops auto-merge becoming blindness.

Load shrinks by three ratchets: contracted endpoints exit review forever; every rejection you make becomes a test plus a machine check so the class never returns; anything escalated once becomes a rule and may not come back as a question.

07 / SEQUENCE

Six weeks, then the pilot

Week 1

Take the front door

DNS → Traefik → Fig → legacy at 100% passthrough (rollback drilled first). Emergency authz on admin/refund routes; WS identity at handshake. PII redaction human-reviewed, then the recorder turns on. QPS validation gate — the go/no-go on this entire spine.

Week 2

Walking skeleton + corpus of truth

Golden-path Go service template + first service (read-only catalog) through Traefik, 2 replicas, kill-a-replica green. Generated client v0. Bug Ledger seeded with ~30 failing tests. closure.yaml emits its first red cells.

Week 3

First endpoints contracted

Menu + feed walk the full ladder to contracted. Promotion Cards and auto-rollback live. Design system v0: DTCG tokens → four surfaces. First burndown number published.

Week 4

The Stepper shadows Temporal

~400-line saga package with deadline rows and declared compensations; kill-tests at 9 crash points. Runs read-only, diffing intended transitions against Temporal daily. Fig identity broker live.

Week 5

Dispatch and realtime

New Go WS gateway with event-sequence parity; 5% of riders on the new URL. Location + availability contracted. Order-tracking screen with Empty/Slow/Broken gating merge.

Week 6

Checkout in shadow; human loop closes

Compute-only checkout shadow — decisions logged, not performed; server-authoritative pricing behind it (legacy diffs where legacy is wrong become Bug Ledger rows). First briefing generated, first human-aimed seed. Scope-cut list signed.

Wks 7–8

Stepper takes new orders

Divergence ≈ 0 → actuator for new orders; Temporal drains. Checkout canary to internal users. Money-closure query green; payout ledger fills its matrix cells.

Wks 9–10

PILOT — real money

One restaurant, 3–5 riders, ~30 invited customers, live paid orders 100% on the new stack. Recorded unaided sessions with real people. Auto-rollback on every pilot route.

M4+

Burndown to cutover

Per-context contract deadlines dated at each context's start. UI rolls out screen-by-screen gated on funnel telemetry. Auth strangled last. Temporal deleted at last-workflow-complete. DB contract steps last, human-gated.

09 / DISCARDED

What each plan lost

Cut wholesale

  • B's simulator — 4–6k permanent lines, an architecture tax on every feature, and a failure mode (a lagging sim that lies with authority) that is disqualifying. Its ideas survive in boring form.
  • D's metamodel + six generators — the most irreversible front-loaded decision of the six, with a documented death spiral and system-wide unreviewed blast radius. Only the existing buf pipeline and design tokens survive.
  • E's org costume — nine roles, charters, memory files, WIP limits, andon cords, at 3–8× token cost. Its ~8 mechanical checks are kept; the costume is deleted.
  • F's prose apparatus — readability lints, glossaries, novice quizzes, calibration ledgers. Fluency and truth are uncorrelated in exactly the failure cases that matter.

Duplicates resolved

  • Bug corpus — four plans proposed the same thing. One Bug Ledger survives.
  • Orchestration — six proposals; three independently converged on a deadline-mandatory schema. That convergence was the strongest signal in the exercise, and the smallest implementation won.
  • PR summary — five formats; two cards survive.
  • UI state gate — two identical proposals; one kept.
  • Review audits — both kept, because seeds audit reviewers and sampling audits the auto-merge tier. They don't overlap.

Even the spine lost pieces: rollback drills on already-contracted read-only routes, floating mismatch budgets (replaced by a tighten-only ratchet), any Fig scope beyond record/shadow/identity, and the undated long tail that is where strangler projects historically die.
