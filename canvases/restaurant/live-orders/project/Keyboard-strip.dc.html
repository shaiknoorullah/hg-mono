The stray-key guard

  1. A and D act only on the tile that has keyboard focus. The tile itself is the focus stop (tabindex 0 on the active tile, −1 on the others, named “New order A7K2, 2 minutes 12 seconds left, $37.69”), and the focus ring and “Selected” show which one. There is no page-wide shortcut.
  2. A new order never takes focus. It rings and joins the strip; the kitchen reaches it with Skip to new orders, the Ringing badge or Tab.
  3. When the strip re-sorts (a new order with an earlier deadline arrives), focus stays on the same order, not the same position. The tile under your focus never changes without you moving.
  4. When the focused order ends (timed out, withdrawn, accepted on another screen), focus moves to the tile’s own note, not to the next Accept. The next A press does nothing until you move.
  5. A in a text field (the decline detail) types a letter. Keys only work on a tile.
  6. Decline never completes from the keyboard shortcut: D opens the reason form in the panel, and Decline order still needs a reason.

Header or footer: recommendation

Both are drawn (the footer as an alternative board). Recommended: header. It sits directly under the status bar where the eye lands after the ring, keeps clear of the panel’s action footer and the toasts at the bottom, matches the tablet where the bottom edge is closest to the counter and gets knocked, and keeps Skip to new orders the first stop after the navigation.

The footer keeps the list higher on the page, but splits attention between the top status and the bottom strip and competes with the panel footer for the same corner.

Sizes

  • Accept on every tile is the 72 px critical size; Decline is a 44 px ghost button, 24 px from Accept. On the tablet Decline is a 44 px close icon button (named “Decline order A7K2, choose a reason”), still 24 px away, and the key hints are hidden.
  • Up to three tiles show in full on a 1440 screen and on the 1180 tablet; a fourth sits behind a “+1 more” control that scrolls the strip, so no Accept is ever half hidden.
  • The strip is its own region: it scrolls sideways; the page never scrolls.
