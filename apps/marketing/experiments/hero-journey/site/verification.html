The verification standard.

What a halal certificate has to survive before a kitchen appears on HalalGoes. If we change any of it, the version number changes with it.

01The seven checks

All seven must pass. One fail and the kitchen is not listed — there is no partial credit, no provisional listing, and nothing is approved on a timer. Checks five and seven are computed by the server and cannot be overridden by any member of our team.

  1. 01 · H1_LEGIBLE_COMPLETE The document is readable, whole, and unaltered

    Every page present, nothing obscured, no sign the certificate has been edited. A person looks at the scan.

  2. 02 · H2_ISSUER_ACCEPTED The issuer is one we accept

    The certifying body is on our accepted registry at the moment of review — and we print its name on the listing, so you can judge the issuer for yourself.

  3. 03 · H3_NAME_MATCH The certificate names this business

    The legal name on the certificate is the restaurant’s registered legal name, or a recorded alias of it. Not a parent brand.

  4. 04 · H4_ADDRESS_MATCH The certificate covers this address

    The certified address matches the premises being listed — or the certificate explicitly names multiple premises and this is one of them.

  5. 05 · H5_DATES_VALID It is in force today, with time left on it

    Issued in the past, and not expiring within our minimum remaining window. The server computes this. No admin can wave it through.

  6. 06 · H6_SCOPE_SUFFICIENT It covers the food, not just the supplier

    The scope has to cover the whole establishment or the kitchen. A certificate that only covers a meat supplier fails this check — it is the exact thing that gets mistaken for a restaurant being certified.

  7. 07 · H7_UNIQUE_NOT_REUSED It has not been used by anyone else

    The certificate number is not already approved for a different restaurant. Also computed by the server, also not overridable.

02The certifying bodies we accept

A certificate from any one of these satisfies check two. The registry is seeded, not closed — it can be extended, and the reason it exists at all is so that the issuer’s name can be printed on the listing rather than hidden behind a label of ours.

  • Halal Monitoring Authority (HMA Canada) Ontario, Canada
  • Islamic Society of North America Canada (ISNA Canada) Ontario, Canada
  • Halal Food Standards Alliance of America (HFSAA) United States — certificates issued to Canadian establishments are accepted

We are not affiliated with any of them and none of them endorses us. We do not rank, score or editorialise certifying bodies — which body’s standard you accept is your judgement, not ours.

If your issuer isn’t listed

Send the certificate anyway. If the issuing body is accredited and the certificate covers the kitchen, we will assess it and tell you either way, with a reason. We would rather add an issuer we can stand behind than turn away a kitchen that has done the work.

03What the badge can say

  • Halal certified

    A current certificate is on file, a person has checked it against all seven points, and the record says who checked it and when.

    RECORD SHOWN ON THE LISTING
  • Expired

    The restaurant comes off the app until it is renewed. An expired certificate means our information has lapsed — not that anything is wrong with the food. There is no grace period.

    DELISTED WITHIN 60 SECONDS
  • Under review

    We have the paperwork and we have not finished checking it. Nothing is ever auto-approved on a timer.

    NOT VISIBLE TO CUSTOMERS
  • No record

    If we have not read a certificate, you see nothing at all. Never a maybe, never an optimistic badge.

    NO BADGE RENDERED

No halal state is ever shown in red. Red reads as haram — a religious ruling we do not make. An expired certificate is shown in slate, and it means our information has lapsed, not that anything is wrong with the food.

04What we explicitly do not claim

  • We do not certify food. We read certificates issued by others.
  • We do not make religious rulings, and we do not adjudicate between schools of thought.
  • We do not rank or score certifying bodies.
  • We do not inspect kitchens, slaughterhouses or supply chains ourselves.
  • We do not verify certificates against a government registry — no such registry exists for halal certification in Canada.
  • We do not accept a supplier’s certificate as evidence that a kitchen is certified.
  • We do not allow a restaurant to pay for or influence its position in the feed.
